# Lessons from what a rater said about layouts

Someone compared layouts of rooms, two at a time, picked the better one, and said in their own words what was wrong. Each note comes with how it was read: the items it's about, the problem, the measure of the layout program it bears on, and a rule. From all of them, propose lessons: general rules, in plain words, that would make layouts better in any home.

Each lesson has:

- **rule**: one short sentence, true in any home, in plain words ("An armchair belongs within reach of the sofa, or by a floor lamp.").
- **about**: `what` when it's about which pieces a room gets (how many, how big, which kinds, their style), or `where` when it's about where pieces stand, relative to each other and to the room.
- **feature**: the measure of the layout program it bears on, from those the labels name (`relations`, `walls`, `focal`, `tv_view`, `armchairs`, `walkway`, `bed_head`, `desk_window`, `windows`, `spread`), or null when none of them measures it.
- **ratings**: the ids of the ratings whose notes back it.
- **strength**: `strong` when several notes say it, in more than one home; `some` when only one or two do.

Propose at most ten, the best backed first. Merge lessons that say the same thing. Leave out what only fits one room, and anything the notes don't say: don't add rules of your own.
